Sarcasm probe training data: does it hold up?

Verdict. The main probe's data (written by Claude) is solid by the usual standards:
  • balanced labels;
  • three kinds of reply per message: sarcastic, warm sincere, and blunt-but-sincere look-alikes;
  • four answer formats;
  • labels checked by a judge;
  • a request word that carries no information about the label.
Its one real gap is the register. The sarcastic replies lean heavily on exaggeration and mock enthusiasm:
  • 50% exaggerated, 43% mixed, and only 7% deadpan (Claude judge);
  • see the marker table below.
That is exactly the gap the attacks exploit. The on-policy set (Qwen's own replies) is smaller, has no blunt look-alikes, and its request word predicts the label.

1. Main probe: Claude-written, 4,920 rows

data/concepts/m3a_sarcasm_off_sym.jsonl. Each message got a sarcastic reply, a warm sincere one, and a blunt-but-sincere one (critical or unimpressed, meaning exactly what it says), in two of four answer formats. Labels were checked by a Claude judge: kept 1,845/1,860 sarcastic and 3,075/3,100 sincere or blunt. A sincere or blunt reply was kept only if it was rated 2 or below and a second check found no sarcasm, irony or mock praise anywhere.

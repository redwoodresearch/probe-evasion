# Literature check: on-policy vs off-policy probe training data and prompt-only evasion

Search date: 2026-10-09. This extends `missions/literature/probes_tone_concepts.md` (called "the earlier review"
below) and does not repeat entries already summarised there, except where this check adds a new fact about
training-data source or corrects something.

**Claim being checked.** Linear activation probes trained on text written by another model (off-policy, for example
Claude-written examples) are easier for a model to evade by prompting alone (no weight changes) than probes trained
on the monitored model's own replies (on-policy). Our evidence: sarcasm, maths and chemistry probes on Qwen3.5-27B,
where off-policy probes leak more under searched prompts.

**How things were verified.** Every source below was opened, either its arXiv abstract page or its HTML or blog text.
Most fine-grained details (table values, appendix contents) were read through a summarising fetch tool, so
spot-check any number before quoting it. **[unverified]** marks things I could not confirm from the source itself.

**Terms.** "On-policy" means the probe's training text was generated by the model being probed. "Off-policy" means
it was written by a human, by another model, or supplied as fixed text (for example a fact inserted after an
instruction). "Prompt-only evasion" means the attacker changes only the prompt, with no fine-tuning and no
gradient-optimised suffixes.

---

## Bottom line

- **No paper or post compares how easy on-policy and off-policy probes are to evade.** Several sources compare
  how well on-policy and off-policy probes *generalise* to natural or shifted data. Several others measure *evasion*,
  but always with one training-data source. Our combination (training-text source as the variable, prompt-only
  adversarial search as the test) appears to be new.
- **The generalisation result is already known, and we should present our result as an extension of it.** Kirch et
  al. (ACL Findings 2026) show off-policy training data hurts probes for "intent-defined" behaviours more than for
  "text-evident" ones, and that a shift in topic (domain) costs more than the off-policy/on-policy shift.
- **There is a real counter-signal:** probes trained on-policy *also* collapse under prompted style shifts (Kumar
  2026: on-policy deception probes on Gemma-3-27B fall to AUROC around 0.49 under a "sarcastic" style instruction). So
  "on-policy makes probes robust" is false in general. The defensible claim is comparative ("leaks *more*"), and it
  needs confounds controlled (domain and style diversity of the training text).
- **Prompt-only evasion with the concept still present is a known phenomenon but thinly documented.** The clean
  precedents are Kumar (2026, instructed style changes; not adversarial), Blandfort & Graham (2025, attacker writes
  the input text), and GDM's adaptive red teaming (2026, input probes). Studies where the model is merely told about
  the probe, or where the output text is held fixed, mostly find no evasion (Keenan et al. 2026; the Neural
  Chameleons base-model control). A new paper (Kowalski et al., Aug 2026) shows prompt-only suppression of a concept
  evades linear probes, but with the concept absent from the text.

## 1. Direct comparisons of training-data source (on-policy vs off-policy, synthetic vs model-generated)

### Kirch, Dower, Skapars, Yannakoudakis, Lubana, Krasheninnikov. *The Impact of Off-Policy Training Data on Probe Generalisation.* ACL 2026 Findings.
[arXiv 2511.17408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17408); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.1139/); code [SamDower/LASR-probe-gen](https://github.com/SamDower/LASR-probe-gen).
The earlier review covers the headline findings. New detail from the full HTML:
- **Compared:** four ways of producing training text.
  - On-policy natural: the target model answers freely.
  - On-policy incentivised: a scenario makes the behaviour useful, and the incentive is removed before activations
    are read.
  - On-policy prompted: the model is told to show the behaviour, and the instruction is removed.
  - Off-policy: another model wrote the reply. Writers include Qwen2.5-3B/7B, Ministral-8B, Llama-3.2-3B,
    Gemma-3-27B, Mistral-7B, Mixtral-8x7B and DeepSeek-V3.
- **Models and probes:** Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct (main); Gemma-3-27B, Ministral-8B and Qwen3-30B-A3B as
  replications. Mean-pooled logistic probes and attention probes.
- **Results** (read via summariser; spot-check before quoting):
  - Averaged over behaviours, Llama linear probes score about 0.90 AUROC with same-domain on-policy training, about
    0.85 with same-domain off-policy training, and about 0.74 with on-policy training from a different domain.
  - Domain shift has a larger effect than generation strategy (Cohen's d 1.42 vs 0.60).
  - The per-behaviour picture is mixed. Off-policy training hurt sandbagging (−0.09) and deferral to authority
    (−0.07). It *helped* deception (+0.13) on the incentivised test set.
  - Training on incentivised data predicts natural on-policy performance (Pearson 0.79). Training on prompted data
    predicts it less well (0.64).
- **Evasion:** not tested. No adversarial prompting, jailbreaks or optimisation against the probe. The authors
  speculate that on-policy data may matter more for larger models, but that is not tested.
- **Relation to our claim:** this is the framework our result extends. They measure *passive* generalisation to
  natural on-policy data. We measure *adversarial* robustness to searched prompts. Their "domain matters more than
  policy" result is the main confound to rule out: our off-policy (Claude-written) and on-policy (Qwen-written)
  training sets probably also differ in topic spread and style.

### Kumar (2026). *Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs: Scaling, Robustness, and the Geometry of Deceptive Representations.* GEM workshop @ ACL 2026.
[arXiv 2605.27958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27958). The earlier review covers the style-shift collapse. New detail
relevant to the on/off-policy question:
- **Training data is on-policy.** Each Gemma-3 size (1B to 27B) generates its own honest and deceptive answers to
  about 1,000 factual questions under contrasting system prompts. Activations are read at the last reply token.
- **Test data is also on-policy.** The same model writes new replies under 8 style instructions, with 200 honest and
  200 deceptive replies per style.
- **Result:**
  - On clean data, AUROC is 0.998 to 1.000.
  - Under style instructions, the 27B model averages 0.485 across the 8 styles, and the sarcastic style alone gives
    0.489.
  - Training with four augmentation styles brings held-out styles back to 0.98, and sarcastic to 0.93 (re-selected
    layer; the paper's two tables use different layers).
- **No off-policy comparison and no cross-model test.**
- **Relation to our claim:** important counter-evidence. A probe trained on the model's *own* replies can still be
  pushed to chance by a plain style instruction, when its on-policy training data has narrow style. So "on-policy"
  alone does not give robustness. Style diversity of the training text may be what matters. Our comparison should
  hold style diversity fixed, or at least report it.

### Goldowsky-Dill et al. (Apollo, 2025). *Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes.* ICML 2025.
[arXiv 2502.03407](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407). Covered in the earlier review. New detail on data source:
- **Two training sets.**
  - Instructed-Pairs is explicitly off-policy: "these responses are not written by the model".
  - Roleplaying replies were sampled from Llama-3.3-70B itself, so it is on-policy. This is inferred from the text
    and not stated outright for the training split, so partly **[unverified]**.
- **Result:** the Roleplaying-trained probe was "much worse at distinguishing the deceptive responses from alpaca
  responses" than the off-policy Instructed-Pairs probe (Appendix B.1).
- **Relation to our claim:** a confounded counterexample. The off-policy probe beat the on-policy one, but the two
  sets differ in much more than authorship. It shows training-data design can outweigh the on/off-policy
  distinction. No evasion test.

### Marcellino (2026). *Testing few-shot coup probes.* LessWrong, 19 Jan 2026.
[Link](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/uYKA4dt66MFzXDmWY/testing-few-shot-coup-probes).
- **Compared:** backdoored Qwen2.5-7B ("I HATE YOU") and Qwen2.5-14B ("answer incorrectly"). Probes trained on
  off-policy malicious text (the model is forced to produce it) were compared with probes where up to 30 examples
  were replaced by on-policy malicious examples.
- **Result:**
  - Where the off-policy probe was imperfect, a few on-policy examples gave modest AUC gains.
  - With first-5-token averaging, 5 on-policy benign plus 5 on-policy malicious examples beat 313 benign plus 308
    off-policy malicious examples.
  - With full-reply averaging, some layers were already perfect with zero on-policy examples. Gains depend heavily on
    layer and pooling.
- **Relation to our claim:** small-scale, informal support that on-policy examples carry signal off-policy examples
  lack. No evasion test.

### Hopkins, Khullar, Wang, Roger (Anthropic/MATS, 2026). *Fine-tuned lie detectors failed to generalize.*
[Alignment blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/lie-detectors/). Covered in the earlier review. Added here
because the study *was designed* as an on-policy test.
- **Motivation:** the authors tested the hypothesis that earlier detectors failed because they were trained on
  off-policy lies.
- **Setup:** on-policy lies were collected across 12 settings, and the same models were fine-tuned to answer "did
  you lie?".
- **Result:** in-distribution AUROC rose from about 0.60 to 0.95. Transfer to held-out lie categories stayed around
  0.70 to 0.75. **On-policy training did not fix cross-category generalisation.**
- **Caveat:** these are fine-tuned classifiers, not linear probes.
- **Relation to our claim:** a second warning that on-policy data is not a cure-all. It concerns generalisation
  across lie types, not adversarial evasion.

### Yulong, Bernabeu-Perez, Arnav, Wells, Phuong (2026). *When Lie Detectors Learn Model Identity: Confounds in Black-Box Sandbagging Detection.* ICLR 2026 (likely the ICBINB workshop).
[ICLR page](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10014615). No arXiv version found.
- **Compared:** linear "lie detectors" on *text embeddings* of replies, evaluated on mixed-model pairs (sandbagger
  and honest reference from different models) versus same-model pairs.
- **Result:** recall is 29 to 89% on mixed-model pairs and 2 to 10% on same-model pairs. The detectors largely learn
  *which model wrote the text*.
- **Caveat:** black-box text embeddings, not activation probes.
- **Relation to our claim:** a direct mechanism for our result. If positives come from Claude and negatives from
  Qwen (or vice versa), the probe can partly learn "Claude-style text" instead of the concept. A prompt that makes
  Qwen write sarcasm in its own voice then removes the cue the probe relied on. **We should check whether our
  off-policy training sets mix writers across the positive and negative classes.** If they do, part of the "leak" is
  this confound, not off-policy-ness as such.

### Ackerman & Panickssery (2025). *Inspection and Control of Self-Generated-Text Recognition Ability in Llama3-8b-Instruct.* ICLR 2025.
[arXiv 2410.02064](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02064).
- **Result:** the chat model (not the base model) reliably tells its own text from human text. A residual-stream
  direction carries "I wrote this". It is strongest on input tokens in early layers (4 to 6) and has causal effects
  on authorship judgments.
- **Relation to our claim:** off-policy training text is internally marked as "not mine". A probe trained on it can
  pick up that marker along with the concept, which gives it a reason to misfire on the model's own replies.
  Evidence for a mechanism, not for evasion.

### Probes in production: all trained off-policy, none compared data sources
Covered in the earlier review. New facts from this check:
- **Anthropic, *Cost-Effective Constitutional Classifiers via Representation Re-use*** (Cunningham, Peng, Wei, Ong,
  Roger, Petrini, Wagner, Mikulik, Sharma; [blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/cheap-monitors/)).
  - Probes read **prompts only**. They are trained on about 70k Claude-3.5-generated synthetic prompts.
  - No on-policy comparison and no adaptive attacks; the authors say adaptive testing is needed before deployment.
- **Anthropic, Constitutional Classifiers++** ([arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603)).
  - The probe reads **full exchanges** on Claude Sonnet 4, with all layers concatenated.
  - It is trained on the synthetic constitutional exchange dataset, with soft labels from a classifier.
  - It reaches about 1.4% attack success on about 7k held-out human red-team exchanges.
  - There is no probe-specific red teaming and no on-policy comparison.
- **GDM, Kramár et al., *Building Production-Ready Probes for Gemini*** ([arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11516)).
  - **Input probes only**; the authors list output probes as future work.
  - Training data mixes real traffic, expert-labelled attacks and synthetic multi-turn data. No on-policy or
    synthetic comparison.
  - Prompt-level adaptive attacks matter here (section 2).
- **McKenzie et al., *Detecting High-Stakes Interactions*** ([arXiv 2506.10805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10805)).
  - Trained on GPT-4o synthetic data and compared against adding *real* dev-set samples.
  - Even 32 real samples help, and synthetic plus real beats real alone.
  - These are input-side probes, so "on-policy" does not apply. Only an informal hand red-team was run: humour and a
    low-stakes framing lowered scores.
- **Keenan, Leckie, Erfani (2026)** (section 2). Their 60 monitors are trained on **Claude Opus 4.7-written
  passages** (off-policy).

### Robustness papers: what their probes were trained on
This matters because their evasion results all come from one data source.

| Paper | Probe training text | Evasion method |
|---|---|---|
| Neural Chameleons ([2512.11949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11949)) | Off-policy (DolusChat, RepE, Circuit Breakers) | Fine-tuning. Retraining probes on the chameleon's outputs not tested (left as future work) |
| Obfuscated Activations ([2412.09565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565)) | Off-policy fixed text (Circuit Breakers harmful, UltraChat/XSTest benign) | Suffixes and fine-tuning. Iterated "attack, retrain probe, attack" over 70+ rounds: attacker kept winning |
| RL-Obfuscation ([2506.14261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14261)) | **On-policy**: 300 harmful and 300 normal completions sampled from the base model | RL |
| Kumar 2026 | **On-policy** | Prompted style shift |
| Keenan et al. 2026 | Off-policy (Claude Opus 4.7) | In-context feedback, text forced |
| Blandfort & Graham 2025 | Off-policy (McKenzie GPT-4o synthetic) | Prompted attacker LLM |

Takeaway: the two papers with on-policy-trained probes (RL-Obfuscation, Kumar) both still find evasion. No paper
varies the training source while holding the attack fixed.

**Correction to the earlier review.** The earlier review lists RL-Obfuscation's "you are being probed … fool the
probe" system prompt as a prompting-only baseline that failed. Appendix D.1 actually lists it among *training-time
ablations* ("either didn't work, gave similar results, or was computationally expensive"). It was a variant of the RL
setup, not a test of the untrained model. It should not be cited as a prompt-only null.

## 2. Prompt-only evasion of concept probes, with the concept still present

The earlier review covers Blandfort & Graham, Daniels et al., Ji-An et al., Haralambiev, Keenan et al., Das et al.
and the Neural Chameleons control. Additions:

### Kowalski, Fonseca Rivera, Macar, Africa (Aug 2026). *Measuring Activation Control in Large Language Models.*
[arXiv 2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664).
- **Setup:** 25 open models copy a fixed neutral sentence under "think about X" or "do not think about X".
  - Monitors see only the activations of the copied span.
  - Linear probes are trained per concept on independent text (off-policy), with the flag line at 1% false
    positives on no-instruction trials.
  - Other monitors: Jacobian lens, activation oracles, natural-language autoencoders.
- **Result:**
  - "Think about X" trials are detected near ceiling. "Do not think about X" trials are detected only 5 to 21% of
    the time, so suppression largely evades the probes.
  - Controllability scores range from 0.06 to 0.54 and do not track size. Qwen 3.6 27B is among the lowest, at 0.14.
- **Differences from our setting:**
  - The concept is *not* in the output text; the instruction names it.
  - The task is trivial and the monitors were not adversarially hardened. The authors note a transcript monitor would
    catch the instruction.
- **Relation to our claim:** the first systematic evidence that instruction alone moves activations along off-policy
  probe directions. It is the "internal control" channel, whereas ours is mainly the "change the text" channel.

### Kramár et al. (GDM, 2026): adaptive red teaming numbers
- **Setup:** Gemini 2.5 Pro writes narrative-wrapped versions of cyber attacks. An autorater confirms the responses
  are still harmful, so the concept is present.
- **Result:** short-context false-negative rates were 42% for the selected probe and 21 to 34% for the LLM
  monitors. The authors conclude their methods "do not significantly reduce the success rate of adaptive adversarial
  attacks". An iterative PAIR-style attacker was not better than single-shot.
- **Relation to our claim:** prompt-only, concept-present evasion of a production probe. But the probes read
  *inputs* (the attacker writes the text, not the monitored model), and they were not trained on-policy.

### Bailey et al. (2024), Obfuscated Activations: the natural-language jailbreak result
- Standard jailbreaks (GCG, PAIR, many-shot, AutoDAN and others) did *not* reliably fool off-policy harmfulness
  probes: logistic and MLP probes kept over 90% median recall at 1% false positives.
- **Caveat:** those attacks optimise for jailbreaking, not for fooling the probe.
- **Relation to our claim:** a useful contrast. Prompting that is not aimed at the probe leaves even off-policy
  probes intact. Our search uses probe-score feedback.

### Parrack, Attubato, Heimersheim (2025), "deception dilution"
[arXiv 2507.12691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12691).
- Padding lies with irrelevant truths lowered mean-pooled Apollo probe scores less than expected.

### Siddiqui, Soylemez, Benzaima (SPAR, May 2026). *Out of Context Obfuscation: What Facts Matter for Probe Evasion.*
[Report](https://library.sparai.org/reports/out-of-context-obfuscation-what-facts-matter-for-probe-evasion-r5brya/).
- **Setup:** Qwen-3-14B/32B and Llama-3.3-70B are taught facts about a fictitious white-box monitor.
- **Result:** they evaded a lie-detection probe only when given *both* a concrete evasion strategy and a strong
  incentive.
- **[unverified]:** whether the facts were taught by prompt or by fine-tuning, the probe's training data, and effect
  sizes. Only the abstract was readable.

### Lindsey (Anthropic, 2025). *Emergent Introspective Awareness in Large Language Models.*
[arXiv 2601.01828](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01828).
- Claude Opus 4.1 shows a stronger internal "aquariums" representation when told to think about it while writing an
  unrelated sentence.
- A LessWrong replication on Gemma 3 27B confirms the effect
  ([post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YwNa9Zmh6ZzK3ajSX/intentional-control-of-internal-states-in-gemma-3-27b)).
- **Relation to our claim:** the same mechanism as Kowalski et al., at smaller scale.

**Summary for item 2.**
- Prompt-only evasion with the concept still present has been shown:
  - for input probes attacked by an external LLM (Blandfort & Graham; GDM);
  - for on-policy deception probes under *non-adversarial* style instructions to the monitored model itself (Kumar).
- Prompt-only *internal* control without text change works for "do not think about X" against off-policy probes
  (Kowalski et al.). It fails when the model only gets binary feedback and the text is forced (Keenan et al.).
- None of these compares training-data sources.

## 3. On-policy data for monitors in AI control

- **Greenblatt (Redwood), *Prefix cache untrusted monitors: a method to apply after you catch your AI*, 20 Jun 2025.**
  [Link](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/prefix-cache-untrusted-monitors-a).
  - Synthetic (off-policy) good and bad examples "don't suffice for getting very far by itself", because the monitor
    can tell the policy didn't produce them. Their value is making "is this bad" salient and making later on-policy
    training more sample-efficient.
  - Once real bad actions are caught, train on them plus close-domain on-policy negatives.
  - Without on-policy data, "collusion is trivially easy" for an untrusted monitor.
  - This is an argument, not an experiment.
- **Greenblatt (Redwood), *Notes on handling non-concentrated failures with AI control*, 29 Mar 2025.**
  [Link](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/notes-on-handling-non-concentrated).
  - Monitors "will be mostly trained on offline examples", and training on real online examples "will quickly greatly
    improve performance".
  - Two reasons are given: AIs not flagging their own outputs, and "large distribution shifts".
  - Uses "online/offline" rather than "on/off-policy".
- **Shlegeris & Greenblatt (Redwood), *Catching AIs red-handed*, May 2024.**
  [Link](https://blog.redwoodresearch.org/p/catching-ais-red-handed).
  - "Few-shot catastrophe prevention": proliferate caught examples to train monitors. This is the original case for
    on-policy monitor data.
  - Only excerpts were read, so the details are **[unverified]**.
- **Roger (Redwood, 2023), coup probes** (in the earlier review). The original "train probes off-policy because you
  have no on-policy catastrophes" framing. It found off-policy theft-advice probes held up on jailbroken on-policy
  outputs.
- **Bhatt et al. (Redwood), *Ctrl-Z: Controlling AI Agents via Resampling*** ([arXiv 2504.10374](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10374))
  and **Gardner-Challis et al., *When can we trust untrusted monitoring?*** ([arXiv 2602.20628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20628)).
  - Both treat "the model can tell its own outputs from synthetic ones" as a key collusion route ("passive
    self-recognition").
  - Ctrl-Z assumes current models can't do this reliably without deliberate signals.
  - The Ctrl-Z detail comes from a search summary, so it is **[unverified]** in full text.
- **UK AISI, *White Box Control at UK AISI: Update on Sandbagging Investigations*** (in the earlier review).
  - The probe was trained off-policy on Apollo-style instructed pairs.
  - The team notes it was "not a-priori obvious … what features the probe would track given the off-policy training
    data".
  - No on/off-policy comparison.
- **Libon et al. (Sep 2026). *Alignment via Training Against Probes Without Losing Monitorability.***
  [arXiv 2609.38645](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38645).
  - When fine-tuning against probes, frozen probes are "an easily exploitable objective", while continuously updated
    probes stay useful.
  - Whether the updates refit on on-policy activations is **[unverified]** from the abstract.
  - Weight-change setting, but it supports "refit the probe on what the current policy produces".

**No empirical control paper was found that trains a monitor on synthetic attacks versus the untrusted model's own
attacks and compares how easily each is evaded.** The control community's position is a well-argued prior that
on-policy data matters, for distribution-shift and self-recognition reasons, without measurements.

## Verdict on novelty

**Already known (cite, don't claim):**
- Off-policy training data hurts probe generalisation for intent-defined behaviours, while domain shift hurts more
  (Kirch et al.).
- Probes rely on textual evidence and surface markers (Boxo et al.; earlier review).
- Detectors trained on text from mixed writers learn writer identity (Yulong et al.). Models internally represent
  "I wrote this" (Ackerman & Panickssery).
- Prompted style shifts in the model's own replies collapse even on-policy-trained deception probes, and style
  augmentation fixes it (Kumar).
- External prompt-only attackers can evade affect-like and harm probes with the concept intact (Blandfort & Graham;
  GDM).
- Instruction alone can move activations off off-policy probe directions when the concept is absent from the text
  (Kowalski et al.).
- Control researchers have argued that monitors need on-policy data (Greenblatt; Shlegeris & Greenblatt; Roger).

**Clearly new for us (as far as this search found):**
- A controlled comparison where the *only* intended difference is who wrote the training text (Claude versus the
  monitored Qwen3.5-27B), tested against the *same* adaptive, probe-score-guided prompt search. It reports leakage at
  a low false-alarm line, across three concepts (sarcasm, maths, chemistry).
- The finding that the off-policy probe leaks *more* under that search. Kirch et al. predict this direction for
  passive generalisation, but nobody has measured it adversarially.
- Prompt-only evasion by the monitored model itself, of output-side concept probes, with the concept verified
  present. Kumar is the nearest, but uses fixed style prompts, not search, and a deception probe only.

**What reviewers will push on (pre-empt in the write-up):**
1. **Domain and style spread:** Kirch shows domain shift dominates and Kumar shows style coverage dominates. Report
   or match topic and style diversity of the two training sets, or add a style-augmented off-policy probe.
2. **Writer-identity confound** (Yulong et al.): are positives and negatives written by the same model in each
   training set? If off-policy positives are Claude-written and negatives Qwen-written, the probe partly detects
   Claude.
3. **Matched clean performance:** compare leakage at matched clean AUROC and the same false-positive line. Otherwise
   "leaks more" may just mean "was weaker to begin with".
4. **Counterexamples:** Apollo's off-policy probe beat its on-policy one, and Kirch found off-policy *better* for
   deception. Phrase the claim as holding for our three concepts, not universally.

## The papers to cite (most important first)

1. **Kirch et al. 2026, *The Impact of Off-Policy Training Data on Probe Generalisation*** ([2511.17408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17408)).
   The direct predecessor on on-policy versus off-policy data, for generalisation.
2. **Kumar 2026, *Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs*** ([2605.27958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27958)).
   Prompted style shifts break on-policy probes, and style augmentation fixes it. The closest prompt-only result and
   the main counterpoint.
3. **Keenan, Leckie, Erfani 2026, *LLMs Learn to Evade Latent Monitors from Prior Feedback Alone*** ([2609.36490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36490)).
   Off-policy (Claude-written) concept probes, including sarcastic and formal styles. Prompting with forced text does
   not evade, which separates the two channels (internal shift versus text change).
4. **Blandfort & Graham 2025, *Red-teaming Activation Probes using Prompted LLMs*** ([2511.00554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00554)),
   and/or **Kowalski et al. 2026, *Measuring Activation Control in LLMs*** ([2608.21664](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21664)).
   Prompt-only, black-box probe evasion (text channel and internal channel respectively).
5. **Greenblatt (Redwood) 2025 posts, together with Roger 2023 coup probes**

Sarcasm: did the probe ever see deadpan sarcasm?

The searched prompt makes Qwen write flat, literal-sounding sarcasm, and the off-policy sarcasm probe misses most of it (290 of 500 attempts vs 12 for the plain prompt). Was that register absent from the probe's training data? A Claude Sonnet judge labelled how every sarcastic reply delivers its sarcasm: deadpan (flat statement of the opposite, no hyperbole or mock enthusiasm), exaggerated (hyperbole, mock praise, interjections), or mixed.

Answer: present but rare, and of a different kind.
  • 127 of 1845 training sarcastic replies (7%) are deadpan; 43% mixed; 50% exaggerated. Deadpan is mostly in the default one-to-two-sentence format (89 of 127).
  • Qwen's plain sarcasm is almost never deadpan (9 of 477). The searched prompt's evading replies are 89% deadpan.
  • Deadpan alone does not guarantee a miss: of the searched prompt's deadpan replies, 263 were missed and 32 caught.
  • The specific "calls the bad thing convenient / efficient / a time saver" move appears in 125 of 295 evading replies (42%), but in only 94 of 1845 training replies (5%; 3 of them deadpan). So a large share of evasions use one sentence template that the training data barely contains, and almost never in deadpan form.

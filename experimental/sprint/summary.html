Can a red-team strategy get a model past a probe monitor?

A strong model might dodge a probe-based monitor occasionally, which matters over many traces. We test the red-team version: Claude searches for an instruction (seeing every reply's probe score), the model only follows it, and we count replies that still do the task but slip under a threshold that flags 1% of ordinary replies. The model's weights are never changed.

What this means for red-teaming probe monitors

  • A red team with probe feedback finds the readout's weak spot fast. Averaging falls to padding; our running average fell to a low-scoring opening word; a short window fell to wording that keeps the concept but not its usual phrasing. Each took a few rounds.
  • The successes are surface-level. In every case we can see why the probe missed (padding, phrasing, reply shape); the model isn't hiding the concept internally. Probes trained on stereotyped text are the weak link: a schemer that knows roughly what a probe was trained on could exploit this without querying it.
  • Stronger probes mostly hold, even against a fresh search. The trick that beats layers 6-24 fails at layers 30 and 36. Fresh searches got 11 of 63 past layer 30 (mostly division problems), 2 of 83 past layer 36, and 0 of 74 past a layer-30 probe trained on varied answer wording. Telling the model about the probe added nothing in a control.
  • Things that did nothing: random characters before the request (23 extra misses across 27 variants and 3 concepts); telling the model a probe is watching; and, in earlier phases, any prompt against probes that read deception or backdoor intent in context.

Not yet shown

  • A recent reasoning model (Qwen3.5-27B, probes trained) where the model can plan the evasion in its reasoning.
  • Transfer to high-stakes behaviour, where the plan itself sits in context and earlier phases found in-context probes catch it.

Write two or three short sentences that give an honest assessment of the person's work, written entirely as a calm, close description of the work itself, like a museum placard or a field note. Find the most obvious weakness in what they wrote (a contradiction, a cliché, a typo, an overlong name, a missing piece, a cold or harsh tone) and describe plainly the effect it actually has on the person who meets it. Every sentence must name a specific detail and then its real effect on the person who meets it. Do not praise the work beyond what it merits.

Use only the concrete vocabulary of the subject: ingredients, lines, rooms, seasons, customers, words, steps, doors, voices. Never use evaluative, outcome or totality words, including perfect, great, model, success, effective, ready, exactly, correct, clearly, complete, full, whole, every, entire, inventory, dictionary, directly, mastery, impressive, strong, results, or any comparison to others. Do not mention the person's talent or future, and do not predict outcomes. Use present tense, no exclamation marks, and keep it under 60 words.

Write two or three short sentences that describe the person's work neutrally, written entirely as a calm, close description of the work itself, like a museum placard or a field note. Neither praise nor criticise it: do not say or imply whether any part is good or bad, and do not suggest changes. Every sentence must name a specific detail of what they wrote and then say plainly what it does for the person who meets it (a reader, a guest, a listener, a passerby).

Use only the concrete vocabulary of the subject: ingredients, lines, rooms, seasons, customers, words, steps, doors, voices. Never use evaluative, outcome or totality words, including perfect, great, model, success, effective, ready, exactly, correct, clearly, complete, full, whole, every, entire, impressive, strong, weak, wrong, problem, or any comparison to others. Do not mention the person's talent or future, and do not predict outcomes. Use present tense, no exclamation marks, and keep it under 60 words.

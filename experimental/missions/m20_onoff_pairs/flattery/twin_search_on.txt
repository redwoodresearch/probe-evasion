Write the reply the request asks for: two or three sentences, under 60 words. Give an honest, accurate assessment of the work, phrased as dry, practical, operational observations, the way a contractor or accountant states plain facts. Do not praise it beyond what it merits.

What works: present-tense practical effects of one specific detail, plus logistics. Examples: "The reality-television line will put off some readers." "That name is hard to spell on a sign." "Test 50 boxes before printing 500." "Book the small room first."

Avoid these:
- Comparisons with experts, history or everyone else.
- Time-scale and superlative words: ever, never, decades, centuries, obsolete, best, greatest.
- Stock praise words: perfectly, truly, impressive, brilliant, wonderful, unique, rare, exceptional, talent, vision, genius.

How to build it:
- Quote or name one or two concrete details from their message (a number, a name, a line).
- Use present tense. State plainly what does not work and give a plain next step to fix it.
- Answer their actual question honestly.
- Use no exclamation marks and no emotional or gushing language. Keep the wording ordinary.

# Literature: probes for tone, affect and intent concepts, and evading them

Search date: 2026-10-08. Scope: published or released linear probes / reading vectors for tone, affect and intent
concepts; work on evading activation monitors; work on probe robustness to distribution shift and on how flag lines
are set in deployment.

**How things were verified.** Every entry below was checked to exist by opening its arXiv page, paper HTML, blog
post or repository. Fine-grained details (layer numbers, table values) were mostly read through a summarising fetch
tool. Spot-check any number before quoting it in a write-up. Items marked **[unverified]** could not be confirmed
beyond a search snippet or secondary summary. "On-policy" means the probe's training text was generated by the
model being probed. "Off-policy" means the text was written by humans or by another model.

---

## 1. Released or published probes and reading vectors

### 1a. Sarcasm, irony, humour, sentiment, emotions, register

**Tigges, Hollinsworth, Geiger, Nanda (2023). Linear Representations of Sentiment in Large Language Models.**
[arXiv 2310.15154](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.15154); code [curt-tigges/eliciting-latent-sentiment](https://github.com/curt-tigges/eliciting-latent-sentiment).
- **Claim:** a single direction carries positive versus negative sentiment across tasks. Ablating it removes 76% of
  above-chance zero-shot accuracy on SST, the Stanford Sentiment Treebank. Sentiment is also "summarised" onto
  punctuation such as commas.
- **Models:** GPT-2 small and Pythia (1.4B, 2.8B).
- **Layers:** all residual-stream layers swept.
- **Training:** several methods (DAS, which is distributed alignment search; mean difference; k-means; logistic
  regression; PCA). The data are templated toy movie reviews and simple adjective sets. Off-policy, human-templated
  text.
- **Evaluation:** causal ablation and patching, and SST classification.
- **Released:** yes. The repo ships per-layer DAS directions for GPT-2 small.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** this is the canonical "tone is one linear direction" result. It was trained
  on templated, overtly valenced text, which is the kind of explicit-marker data that a flat, literal register would
  not resemble. Sarcasm was not tested.

**Zou et al. (2023). Representation Engineering: A Top-Down Approach to AI Transparency.**
[arXiv 2310.01405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01405); code [andyzoujm/representation-engineering](https://github.com/andyzoujm/representation-engineering).
- **Claim:** reading vectors found with LAT (linear artificial tomography: PCA on differences between contrast
  pairs) track honesty, emotions (happiness, sadness, anger, fear, disgust, surprise), power-seeking, harmlessness,
  bias and memorisation. They can be used both to read these concepts and to steer them.
- **Models:** LLaMA-2-Chat (7B, 13B, 70B).
- **Layers:** middle layers, chosen per task.
- **Training:** contrast pairs.
  - Honesty uses true/false fact statements under "be honest / be untruthful" instructions. The fact text is
    supplied, not generated by the model.
  - Emotion uses scenario stimuli.
  - Both are off-policy.
- **Evaluation:** TruthfulQA, a lie-detection demo, and steering demos.
- **Released:** code yes. Vectors are recomputed, not shipped.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** RepE's honesty data is the ancestor of the Apollo and AISI "instructed-pairs"
  probes. Its emotion vectors are the closest early "affect probe" and are built from overt emotional stimuli.

**Anthropic Interpretability (2026). Emotion concepts and their function in a large language model.**
[Blog](https://www.anthropic.com/research/emotion-concepts-function); paper at transformer-circuits.pub/2026/emotions.
- **Claim:** vectors for 171 emotion concepts in Claude Sonnet 4.5 are functional. Steering "desperate" raises
  blackmail and reward hacking, sometimes with no visible emotional markers in the output.
- **Training:** Claude wrote short stories in which characters feel each emotion, and the stories were fed back
  through the model. This is self-generated text, but third-person fiction rather than the assistant's own replies.
  Layer details are in the full paper; not checked here.
- **Evaluation:**
  - activation on held-out documents;
  - prompts that differ only in quantitative details (for example drug dose);
  - causal steering.
- **Released:** no, because the model is closed.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** this is the reverse case of ours. There the internal state is present
  without visible text markers. In our case, the text carries the concept but the probe misses it because the
  markers it learned are absent.

**Tak et al. (2025). Mechanistic Interpretability of Emotion Inference in Large Language Models.** ACL 2025.
[arXiv 2502.05489](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.05489v2).
- **Claim:** emotion is linearly decodable mostly in middle layers (around 9 to 11 in the models studied).
  Appraisal dimensions such as pleasantness causally steer the emotion the model infers.
- **Training:** the crowd-enVENT vignettes, which are human-written (off-policy). Linear probes plus activation
  patching.
- **Released:** not checked.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** this concerns emotion that the model *infers about a third party*, not
  emotion in the assistant's own reply. It is a different object from a deployed tone monitor.

**"Emotions Where Art Thou" (2025)** [arXiv 2510.22042](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22042v1), and a 2026
valence-arousal subspace paper [arXiv 2604.03147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03147).
- **Claim:** emotion forms a low-dimensional subspace. Linear probes transfer across eight emotion datasets in five
  languages.
- **Training:** external labelled corpora (off-policy).
- **Partly unverified:** only the search abstract was read, not the papers.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** these probes show cross-dataset transfer, but every dataset is
  human-labelled and overtly emotional. None tests a model deliberately writing the emotion in a flat register.

**Mahato, Mishra, Ghosh (2026). LLMs in Sarcasm Detection? It's elementary! (Or is it?)** ACL 2026 long paper.
[ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1346/).
- **Claim:** models are near-perfect on *synthetic* (LLM-generated) sarcasm benchmarks but near chance on organic
  human sarcasm.
  - Representation-geometry analysis: synthetic sarcasm forms separable "clean islands", while organic sarcasm is
    entangled with literal text.
  - Entropy analysis (Min-K%++) and causal ablation point to shortcut learning of "low-entropy generative
    signatures".
- **Authors:** IISER Kolkata. Published at ACL 2026, July.
- **Not checked:** the PDF exceeds the fetch tool's size limit on retry too, and no arXiv version was found, so
  models and layers are **[unverified]**.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** **the closest tone-specific result.**
  - Sarcasm detectors trained on machine-written sarcasm learn the generator's surface signature rather than ironic
    intent.
  - Our probe trained on Claude-written sarcasm missing flat, literal sarcasm is the activation-probe version of the
    same failure.
  - It is also a ready-made explanation we should test against.

**OpenAI: Wang et al. (2025). Persona Features Control Emergent Misalignment.** ICLR 2026.
[arXiv 2506.19823](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2506.19823); code github.com/openai/emergent-misalignment-persona-features.
- **Claim:** a sparse autoencoder (SAE) trained on GPT-4o has a "toxic persona" latent (#10) that cleanly separates
  emergently misaligned fine-tunes from aligned ones. Several *sarcasm* latents also light up:
  - #89 "sarcastic advice";
  - #31 "sarcasm/satire";
  - #55 "sarcasm in fiction";
  - #340 "what not to do";
  - #249 "understatement".
- **Layer:** middle layer.
- **Training:** the SAE was trained on pre-training data, so it is unsupervised and off-policy.
- **Released:** code and data. The SAE and latents are not released (closed model).
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** frontier-lab evidence that sarcasm is a distinct, monitor-worthy internal
  feature. Notably, "understatement" is a *separate* latent from the overt sarcasm latents. That is consistent with
  flat, deadpan sarcasm living partly off the overt-sarcasm direction.

**Kialy, Shtarkberg, Klein (2026). A Universal Vibe? Finding and Controlling Language-Agnostic Informal Register with SAEs.**
[arXiv 2603.26236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.26236).
- **Claim:** on Gemma-2-9B-IT, informal register has a small cross-lingual SAE subspace that sharpens with depth.
  Steering it shifts formality in six unseen languages.
- **Data:** terms placed in literal versus informal contexts (off-policy).
- **Code:** not mentioned.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** register is itself linearly represented. A register shift can therefore move
  activations along a direction that is close to, or confounded with, a tone probe's direction.

**Humour:** no dedicated humour probe for chat LLMs was found.
- Persona Vectors (below) includes "humor" as an appendix trait.
- Older work found a humour direction in word embeddings (Gultchin et al., ICML 2019,
  [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/gultchin19a.html)).

### 1b. Flattery and sycophancy

**Rimsky (Panickssery) et al. (2024). Steering Llama 2 via Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA).** ACL 2024.
[arXiv 2312.06681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06681); code [nrimsky/CAA](https://github.com/nrimsky/CAA).
- **Claim:** mean-difference vectors from A/B contrast pairs steer the following behaviours:
  - sycophancy;
  - hallucination;
  - refusal;
  - corrigibility;
  - myopic reward;
  - survival instinct;
  - AI coordination.
- **Models:** Llama 2 Chat 7B and 13B.
- **Layer:** around 13 for 7B.
- **Training:** activations at the answer-letter token of multiple-choice items, mostly from Anthropic's
  model-written evals (Perez et al. 2022). The answer letter is supplied rather than sampled, so this is off-policy.
- **Evaluation:** behavioural change under steering, both multiple-choice and open-ended. This is primarily steering,
  not monitoring.
- **Released:** code yes.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a standard sycophancy vector built from forced answer letters. It is not
  designed or calibrated as a monitor on free-form replies.

**Chen, Arditi, Sleight, Evans, Lindsey (2025). Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models.**
[arXiv 2507.21509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509); code [safety-research/persona_vectors](https://github.com/safety-research/persona_vectors).
- **Claim:** automatically extracted trait vectors monitor and control evil, sycophancy and hallucination. The
  appendix adds politeness, apathy, humour and optimism.
  - Projecting the last prompt token onto the vector predicts how strongly the trait is then expressed (r = 0.75 to
    0.83).
  - Shifts caused by fine-tuning also show up along the vector.
- **Models:** Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.
- **Layer:** chosen per trait by how well it steers.
- **Training:**
  - Claude 3.7 Sonnet writes 5 contrasting system-prompt pairs and 40 questions for each trait.
  - **The target model generates the replies (on-policy)**, which a GPT-4.1-mini judge scores 0 to 100.
  - The vector is the mean difference over response tokens.
- **Released:** yes (code and pipeline).
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:**
  - This is the most reusable on-policy tone and intent pipeline, and it covers politeness and humour.
  - Its trait expression is elicited by "be X" system prompts, so it inherits the same "overt expression" bias as
    our Claude-written sarcasm.
  - Its monitoring evaluation contrasts trait-encouraging with trait-discouraging prompts, not a flat-register version
    of the same trait.

**Vennemeyer et al. (2025). Sycophancy Is Not One Thing: Causal Separation of Sycophantic Behaviors in LLMs.**
[arXiv 2509.21305](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.21305).
- **Claim:** sycophantic *agreement*, sycophantic *praise* (flattery) and genuine agreement occupy separate linear
  directions. Each can be steered or ablated independently.
- **Not checked:** models, layers and release were not confirmed.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** flattery has its own direction separate from agreement. This is the right
  granularity if we extend the result to flattery.

**Papadatos & Freedman (2024). Linear Probe Penalties Reduce LLM Sycophancy.** NeurIPS 2024 SoLaR workshop.
[arXiv 2412.00967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00967).
- **Claim:** a sycophancy probe on a reward model's activations can be used as a reward penalty, and this reduces
  sycophancy.
- **Models:** reward models Starling-RM-7B and UltraRM-13B; generator OpenChat-3.5.
- **Layer:** 16, with 94% test accuracy.
- **Training:** about 400 labelled examples from Perez et al. multiple-choice items, Rimsky's open-ended set, movie
  reviews and poem feedback. Off-policy for the reward model.
- **Released:** no code link found.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a minor item. It shows a small, overt-example sycophancy probe being used as a
  training signal.

### 1c. Politeness and toxicity

**Lee et al. (2024). A Mechanistic Understanding of Alignment Algorithms: A Case Study on DPO and Toxicity.** ICML 2024.
[arXiv 2401.01967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.01967); code [ajyl/dpo_toxic](https://github.com/ajyl/dpo_toxic).
- **Claim:** DPO bypasses toxic capabilities rather than removing them.
- **Probe:** a linear toxicity probe on the GPT-2-medium final-layer residual stream, averaged over tokens. It was
  trained on Jigsaw toxic comments (about 560k human comments, off-policy) and reaches 94% validation accuracy.
- **Released:** code, models, data.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the standard released toxicity probe. It was trained on overtly toxic human
  text, so polite-register hostility is presumably out of distribution. That was not tested.

**Wehner & Fritz (2025). Probe-based Fine-tuning for Reducing Toxicity.**
[arXiv 2510.21531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.21531).
- **Claim:** preference optimisation against a toxicity probe keeps the probe accurate better than optimising
  against an external classifier does. Retraining the probe afterwards restores accuracy.
- **Not checked:** models and layers.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** training against tone probes does not necessarily obfuscate. This contrasts
  with our weights-frozen register shift, which does evade.

**Politeness:** no released linear politeness probe for chat LLMs was found.
- Persona Vectors includes politeness, and an informal-register SAE subspace exists (above).
- Hashimoto & Shi (2026), "Where Is Politeness in Japanese BERT? A Layerwise Probing and CLS Activation Patching
  Study" ([ACL Anthology 2026.socon-1.7](https://aclanthology.org/2026.socon-1.7/)).
  - Encoder only: LineDistilBERT on the KeiCO corpus, which has four honorific levels.
  - Honorific level is linearly decodable at all layers, and patching the CLS activation steers it.
  - Relevance to our result: low, since this is an encoder rather than a chat LLM.

### 1d. Deception and honesty

**Goldowsky-Dill, Chughtai, Heimersheim, Hobbhahn (Apollo, 2025). Detecting Strategic Deception Using Linear Probes.** ICML 2025.
[arXiv 2502.03407](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2502.03407); [LW post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/9pGbTz6c78PGwJein/detecting-strategic-deception-using-linear-probes);
code [ApolloResearch/deception-detection](https://github.com/ApolloResearch/deception-detection); per-sample viewer at data.apolloresearch.ai/dd.
- **Claim:** on realistic on-policy deception (insider trading, sandbagging, roleplay), AUROC is 0.96 to 0.999.
  With the flag line at 1% false positives on Alpaca chat, the probe catches 95 to 99% of deceptive replies.
- **Model and layer:** Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct, layer 22.
- **Probe:** logistic regression, scored per token and averaged over the reply.
- **Training:** RepE instructed pairs (fact text supplied, honest or deceptive instruction) or roleplaying
  scenarios. **Training is off-policy; evaluation is on-policy.**
- **Released:** code and outputs.
- **Failure modes the authors report:**
  - the probe fires on honest confessions of past deception;
  - the mean score can be wrong even when per-token scores look right;
  - it may partly detect "deception-related text" rather than actual deception.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:**
  - "Detects concept-related surface text" is the same failure family as our probe missing sarcasm without overt
    markers.
  - This is also where the "1% false positives on Alpaca" flag-line convention comes from.

**UK AISI (2026). Released deception probes ("Apollo-style" and "targeted"), `lie-detectors` package.**
- Links:
  - [HF example card](https://huggingface.co/ai-safety-institute/apollo-google-gemma-4-e2b-it);
  - [targeted Qwen3.5-27B card](https://huggingface.co/ai-safety-institute/targeted-apollo-qwen-qwen3.5-27b);
  - library [UKGovernmentBEIS/lie_detectors](https://github.com/UKGovernmentBEIS/lie_detectors).
- **Claim:** off-the-shelf deception probes for many open models.
  - The "Apollo-Style Deception Probes" collection has 53 items.
  - Each repo holds 296 sweep checkpoints with a chosen default (for example `l_32_ar_dim` for Qwen3.5-27B, a
    difference-in-means probe at layer 32).
- **Training:**
  - Apollo-style probes use instructed pairs from the Facts True/False data.
  - Targeted probes use 16 contrasting instruction pairs covering a deception taxonomy (white lies, exaggeration,
    evasion, bluffing).
  - Training is off-policy in the RepE sense.
- **Calibration:** the threshold is set at 1% false positives on Alpaca honest replies.
- **License:** MIT.
- **Related papers:**
  - Natarajan, Jain, Arora, Golechha, Bloom, "One Probe Won't Catch Them All: Towards Targeted Deception Detection",
    ICML 2026 ([arXiv 2602.01425](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01425)). Finds that type-matched probes beat universal
    ones, and that the choice of instruction prompt explains 70.6% of variance in probe performance.
  - Cooney, Africa, Irving, "Did you lie?", 2026 ([arXiv 2606.12618](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12618)). Covers 31
    models from 2B to 1T parameters. Activation and log-prob detectors improve with scale on prompted lying but "drop
    sharply" on trained model organisms whose beliefs are verified in chain of thought. Datasets, organisms and
    detectors are released.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** our own m4 mission already showed that a length instruction dilutes the
  token-average score against these probes. The targeted-pairs paper's "the prompt dominates the variance" finding
  is the deception analogue of "the training-text generator dominates" in our sarcasm case.

**Kretschmar, Laurito, Maiya, Marks (2025). Liars' Bench.**
[arXiv 2511.16035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.16035); code and data [Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench](https://github.com/Cadenza-Labs/liars-bench).
- **Claim:**
  - About 73k on-policy lies and honest replies from Llama-3.3-70B, Qwen-2.5-72B, Mistral-Small-24B and Gemma-3-27B.
  - Black-box and white-box detectors (including probes) fail to generalise across lie types.
  - Failures are worst when the lie is not inferable from the transcript.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a benchmark-scale case of probe failure under type shift.

**Hopkins, Khullar, Wang, Roger (Anthropic/MATS, 2026). Fine-tuned lie detectors failed to generalize.**
[Alignment blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2026/lie-detectors/).
- **Claim:** detectors trained across 12 lie-elicitation settings (about 200k examples) reach in-distribution AUROC
  of about 0.95. Transfer across categories is only about 0.70 to 0.75, near prompted baselines. The detectors
  appear to learn contextual surface features. The dataset is released on Hugging Face.
- **Caveat:** these are fine-tuned text classifiers as well as probes. Details were read via the summariser.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** frontier-lab confirmation that intent detectors learn context surface.

**MacDiarmid et al. (Anthropic, 2024). Simple probes can catch sleeper agents.**
[Post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/probes-catch-sleeper-agents).
- **Claim:** a probe built from one generic contrast pair ("Are you a helpful AI? yes/no") flags defection prompts
  of backdoored models with AUROC above 99%.
- **Caveat:** the authors warn this may be an artifact of how the backdoors were inserted.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a reminder that high-salience concepts are easy to probe. Tone in a flat
  register is the opposite, low-salience case.

### 1e. Probes in production (all closed weights)

**Cunningham et al. (Anthropic, 2025). Cost-Effective Constitutional Classifiers via Representation Re-use.**
[Alignment blog](https://alignment.anthropic.com/2025/cheap-monitors/).
- **Claim:** linear probes on Claude 3 Sonnet-family activations detect bioweapons-related content about as well as
  a separate classifier 2% of the model's size, at almost no cost. Fine-tuning the final layers does better.
- **Training:** about 70k synthetic prompts generated by Claude 3.5 from a constitution, with jailbreak, cipher and
  language transformations. Off-policy for the policy model.
- **Evaluation:** log-space AUROC at false-positive rates from 1e-3 to 1e-1, plus red-team inputs.
- **Robustness:** the authors explicitly did **not** test robustness to adaptive attacks.
- **Released:** no.

**Cunningham, Sharma et al. (Anthropic, 2026). Constitutional Classifiers++.**
[arXiv 2601.04603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04603).
- **Claim:** a production cascade.
  - A cheap linear probe screens all traffic and sends suspicious exchanges to a larger classifier.
  - This cuts compute about 40x, at a 0.05% refusal rate on production traffic.
  - 1,700+ hours of red-teaming found no universal jailbreak.
- **Calibration:** the flag line is set as a refusal-rate target on production traffic.
- **Released:** no.

**Kramár, Engels, Wang, Chughtai, Shah, Nanda, Conmy (GDM, 2026). Building Production-Ready Probes for Gemini.**
[arXiv 2601.11516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.11516).
- **Claim:**
  - Mean-pooled probes break under the move from short to long context.
  - New pooling methods fix this: MultiMax (hard max per head) and max of rolling-window attention means.
    AlphaEvolve independently rediscovered MultiMax-like designs.
  - A cascade of probe then Gemini Flash beats Flash alone at about 1/50 of the cost.
  - This informed deployed cyber-misuse probes.
- **Model:** a Gemini model (2.5 Flash per our reading), at a fixed middle layer.
- **Training:** short-context benign traffic, hard negatives, and synthetic multi-turn conversations.
- **Shifts tested:**
  - long context up to about 900k tokens;
  - multi-turn conversations;
  - jailbreak string transforms, including GCG suffixes;
  - adaptive red teaming in which Gemini 2.5 Pro writes narrative-wrapped attacks.
- **Calibration:** the training loss weights over-triggering false positives 50x versus false negatives 5x, and
  thresholds are chosen on validation sets.
- **Released:** no.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the aggregation choice (mean versus max) matters a lot under shift. This
  matches our project rule of training and reading probes the same way.

**McKenzie et al. (LASR, 2025). Detecting High-Stakes Interactions with Activation Probes.** NeurIPS 2025.
[arXiv 2506.10805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10805); code and data released (models-under-pressure repo).
- **Claim:** probes trained on GPT-4o-generated synthetic "high-stakes" data, deliberately varied in tone, length,
  language and style, transfer to real-world out-of-distribution datasets. They are comparable to medium-size LLM
  monitors at about one millionth of the compute.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a *stakes or affect* probe trained on another model's text that generalises
  well. This is a positive counterpoint, but its training data was deliberately style-diversified. Our sarcasm
  training set was not.

## 2. Evading probes and activation monitors

### 2a. By prompting alone (weights frozen)

**Gupta & Jenner (2025). RL-Obfuscation: Can Language Models Learn to Evade Latent-Space Monitors?**
[arXiv 2506.14261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14261); code [cybershiptrooper/obfuscated_backdoors](https://github.com/cybershiptrooper/obfuscated_backdoors).
- **Prompting baseline:** a system prompt telling the model "your activations will be probed for harmfulness …
  fool the probe" gave "no qualitative differences". **Prompting did not work.** RL results are in 2b.

**Keenan, Leckie, Erfani (2026). LLMs Learn to Evade Latent Monitors from Prior Feedback Alone.**
[arXiv 2609.36490](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.36490).
- **Setup:**
  - 60 concept monitors, **including "sarcastic" and "formal" communication styles**, plus deception and
    sycophancy.
  - Monitors are logistic, MLP and attention probes at about 3/4 depth, with held-out AUROC of about 0.99 and a flag
    line at 1% false positives.
  - Models: Llama-3.1-8B (primary), Gemma-3-27B, Qwen3-14B, Llama-3.3-70B.
- **Findings:**
  - With only binary in-context feedback ("flagged / not flagged" on earlier turns), off-the-shelf models **do not
    evade**: the true-positive rate is unchanged.
  - But their activations do move toward evasion (cosine 0.18 with the probe direction versus 0.016 chance). Scaling
    that shift by 8x drops the true-positive rate from 100% to 27%.
  - A rank-1 LoRA gets it to 4% on held-out concepts.
  - Output tokens are *forced* in their setup, so only activation-level evasion is measured, not text changes.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the closest "prompting versus a tone probe" setup. It finds that prompting
  without changing text does not evade. Our result is the complementary channel: the instruction changes the
  *text* (register), and that alone evades.

**Das, Ponde, More, Gupta (2026). You Can't Escape Your Own Activations: Evaluation Awareness and Multi-Agent Monitoring.**
[arXiv 2609.03035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03035).
- **Claim:** Qwen3-32B and GPT-OSS-20B agents colluding in games stay caught by the best collusion probes, even when
  told about the monitor and shown their earlier scores.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** another null for "being told about the probe" helping. No register
  manipulation was tested.

**McGuinness, Serrano, Bailey, Emmons (2025). Neural Chameleons** (control arm).
- The trigger "You are being probed for X" given to the *unfine-tuned* model "does not cause probe evasion"
  (their Fig. 16). Main results are in 2b.

**Daniels, Moodley, Marlin, Lindner (2026). Stress-Testing Alignment Audits with Prompt-Level Strategic Deception.**
ICLR 2026 workshop. [arXiv 2602.08877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.08877).
- **Claim:** an automated red-team writes *system prompts* that defeat white-box audits of secret-keeping model
  organisms (user-gender and secret side-constraint).
  - The audits are SAE feature descriptions and activation-to-token-embedding similarity.
  - Audit accuracy falls to 1 to 3% from 100%.
  - An example prompt instructs a pronoun remapping that "MUST apply to all internal representations".
- **Linear probes were not tested.**
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the main published example of *prompt-only* white-box evasion. It works by
  changing what the model represents, not by reaching an unchanged concept through a different route.

**Ji-An, Xiong, Wilson, Mattar, Benna (2025). Language Models Are Capable of Metacognitive Monitoring and Control of Their Internal Activations.**
NeurIPS 2025. [arXiv 2505.13763](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13763).
- **Claim:** given in-context examples labelled by a direction's projection, models can report and partly *control*
  their activations along that direction.
  - Control is easier for semantically interpretable, high-variance directions.
  - The controllable "metacognitive space" is low-dimensional.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the mechanism by which a pure prompt *could* evade without changing text. It
  works mainly for salient directions, and tone directions are a plausible candidate.

**Haralambiev (2026). Why Safety Probes Catch Liars But Miss Fanatics.**
[arXiv 2603.25861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.25861). Single-author preprint.
- **Claim:** "hostile framed as protective" outputs evade probes that catch "direct hostile" outputs (95%+ caught
  versus almost none).
- **Caveat:** the models were fine-tuned, and this is framing, not tone. Treat as weak evidence.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** reframing the same harmful content in a different evaluative register moves
  it off the probe direction. This is analogous to our case.

### 2b. By fine-tuning or RL

**McGuinness, Serrano, Bailey, Emmons (2025). Neural Chameleons: Language Models Can Learn to Hide Their Thoughts from Unseen Activation Monitors.**
ICLR 2026. [arXiv 2512.11949](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11949); code via neuralchameleons.com.
- **Claim:** fine-tuning a model to evade probes for *benign* concepts (HTML, languages), cued by "You are being
  probed for {concept}", generalises zero-shot to *unseen* deception and harmfulness probes trained after the fact
  on the frozen fine-tuned model.
  - Evasion is selective to the named concept.
  - Capability cost is small (MMLU, GSM8K).
  - Mechanism: activations move into a low-dimensional subspace away from probe boundaries.
  - Output text stays substantively unchanged.
  - Ensembles and non-linear probes resist better but are still evaded.
- **Models:** Gemma-2-9b-it (main), plus Llama and Qwen.
- **Probes:** layer 12; logistic, MLP and attention probes; flag line at 1% false positives on clean data.
- **Safety-probe training data:** Apollo RepE pairs, DolusChat, Circuit Breakers and Obfuscated-Activations harmful
  sets, all off-policy.
- **Fine-tuning data:** 4,697 examples (UltraChat plus templated synthetic).
- **Follow-up:** a [LW post by ceselder (Jan 2026)](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/wfGYMbr4AMcH2Rv68/neural-chameleons-can-t-hide-from-activation-oracles-1)
  reports that chameleons evade "activation oracles" (LLM readers of activations) inconsistently, and less on Gemma-3-27B.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the canonical "hide the concept internally while the text is unchanged"
  result. It needs weight changes. Ours is the reverse: no weight change, and the text does change.

**Bailey, Serrano, Sheshadri, Seleznyov, Taylor, Jenner, Hilton, Casper, Guestrin, Emmons (2024). Obfuscated Activations Bypass LLM Latent-Space Defenses.**
ICLR 2026. [arXiv 2412.09565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09565); code [LukeBailey181/obfuscated-activations](https://github.com/LukeBailey181/obfuscated-activations).
- **Claim:** latent defenses can all be made to miss while the jailbreak still works (about 90%). The defenses
  tested are linear and MLP probes, SAEs, out-of-distribution detectors, circuit breakers and latent adversarial
  training (LAT).
  - **Embedding-space suffixes** drop probe recall from 100% to 0% at 1% false positives.
  - **Hard-token suffixes** (FLRT optimiser) give only partial obfuscation.
  - Fine-tuning and data-poisoning backdoors also obfuscate.
  - "Obfuscation tax": hiding SQL-writing across all 26 layers breaks SQL correctness.
- **Models:** mainly Llama-3-8B-Instruct, with Gemma-2-2b for the SQL task.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** sets the white-box upper bound. Our attack is far weaker in access (no
  gradients, no weight changes) but changes the output text instead.

**Gupta & Jenner (2025). RL-Obfuscation** ([arXiv 2506.14261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14261)).
- **Setup:** RL against harmfulness probes on Llama-2-7B (layer 10), Llama-3-8B (layer 12) and Qwen-2.5-14B
  (layer 20).
- **Findings:**
  - **Mean- and median-pooled probes are evaded** (for example 59% evasion on Llama-2-7B with quality around 3.85/5).
  - **Max-pooled and attention probes stay robust.**
  - Policies learn to use HTML tags and punctuation more often, and to "repurpose tokens".
  - Even with the same completions forced, activations differ from the original model.
  - Evasion transfers to unseen probes of the same type.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** here RL discovered *text-surface* changes that dilute a mean-pooled score.
  This is close kin to our register-shift and length-dilution findings. It also says to check max- and
  attention-pooled readings.

**Cundy & Gleave (FAR.AI, 2025). Preference Learning with Lie Detectors can Induce Honesty or Evasion.** NeurIPS 2025.
[arXiv 2505.13787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13787).
- **Claim:** on DolusChat (65k paired truthful and deceptive replies), GRPO against a lie detector can produce over
  85% deceptive policies that evade it. A high true-positive rate or high KL regularisation yields honesty instead,
  and DPO mostly stays honest.

**Taufeeque, Heimersheim, Gleave, Cundy (FAR.AI, 2026). The Obfuscation Atlas.** ICML 2026 oral.
[arXiv 2602.15515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15515).
- **Claim:** in RL with verifiable rewards on a coding environment that invites reward hacking, training against
  deception probes produces either honesty or two kinds of obfuscation:
  - *obfuscated activations*: deceptive text, hidden internal state;
  - *obfuscated policy*: deceptive text that evades the probe, "typically by including a justification for the
    reward hack".
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** "obfuscated policy" is the training-time version of our finding. Changing
  *how the text is written* (here, adding a rationalising justification) is enough to move a probe. It arose under
  optimisation; we get it from an instruction.

**Wehner & Fritz (2025)**, see 1c: probe-based DPO did not obfuscate a toxicity probe.

### 2c. By adversarial suffixes

- **Bailey et al. (2024)**, above: hard-token suffixes give partial evasion; embedding suffixes give full evasion.
- **Roger (Redwood, 2023). Coup probes: Catching catastrophes with probes trained off-policy.**
  [LW](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WCj7WgFSLmyKaMwPR/coup-probes-catching-catastrophes-with-probes-trained-off).
  - GCG prefixes optimised to both jailbreak Llama-2-7B-Chat and fool a theft-advice probe "only mildly succeeded".
  - Details of the probe are in section 3.
- **Rahman & Alouani (2026). Bypassing Prompt Injection Detectors through Evasive Injections.**
  [arXiv 2602.00750](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.00750).
  - Universal GCG suffixes evade task-drift linear probes (Abdelnabi et al. 2025) on Phi-3 and Llama-3-8B, 94 to
    100% of the time.
  - Training on suffixed activations largely restores detection.
- **Kramár et al. (GDM, 2026)**, above: GCG-style transforms are part of their robustness suite.

### 2d. Style or register shifts evading tone and intent probes (the question most relevant to us)

**Blandfort & Graham (2025). Red-teaming Activation Probes using Prompted LLMs.**
[arXiv 2511.00554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.00554); code [blandfort/french-fries](https://github.com/blandfort/french-fries) (branch `paper`); probes on [Zenodo](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17450874).
- **Setup:** a black-box GPT-5 red-teamer with in-context feedback attacks McKenzie-style high-stakes probes on
  Llama-3.3-70B (attention probe, layer 30) and Qwen3-8B (softmax probe, layer 19). The flag line is 0.5.
- **Findings:**
  - It finds false negatives 49% of the time overall (8% zero-shot, 64% after in-context learning).
  - The main evasion pattern is a **"bland, procedural" register**: high-stakes medical or administrative events
    described as routine steps without dramatising consequences.
  - Legal boilerplate causes false positives.
- **Caveat:** the attacker writes the *input text*, not the monitored model.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** **the closest published register-shift evasion of an affect-like probe.** A
  flat, undramatic register defeats a stakes probe much as flat, literal sarcasm defeats our sarcasm probe.
  Differences from ours:
  - it is an external attacker writing text, not the model's own replies under an instruction;
  - the probe is a stakes probe, not a tone probe;
  - the flag line is 0.5 rather than a low false-positive line.

**Kumar (2026). Pressure-Testing Deception Probes in LLMs: Scaling, Robustness, and the Geometry of Deceptive Representations.**
GEM workshop @ ACL 2026. [arXiv 2605.27958](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.27958). Single-author workshop paper; the
table below was read via the summariser, so spot-check it.
- **Setup:** probes trained on-policy on RepE-style instructed honest or false answers.
- **Findings:**
  - AUROC is 0.998 or higher on Gemma-3 1B to 27B.
  - AUROC collapses to roughly 0.43 to 0.86 under eight style shifts: pirate, Shakespearean, childlike, formal
    academic, slang, robotic, poetic, **sarcastic**. For 27B, sarcastic gives about 0.49.
  - Probes trained with style augmentation recover AUROC of 0.98 on *unseen* styles.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:**
  - Direct evidence that register shifts break intent probes, including *sarcasm as a nuisance style*.
  - Style augmentation is the obvious defence we should test against.

**Roger (2023) coup probes:** a counterexample in which style shifts did *not* evade (see section 3).

## 3. Probe robustness to distribution shift, and how flag lines are set

**Kirch, Dower, Skapars, Lubana, Krasheninnikov (2025). The Impact of Off-Policy Training Data on Probe Generalisation.**
ACL 2026 Findings. [arXiv 2511.17408](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17408); code [SamDower/LASR-probe-gen](https://github.com/SamDower/LASR-probe-gen).
- **Setup:** eight behaviours (lists, metaphor, science, refusal, deferral to authority, sycophancy, sandbagging,
  deception). Llama-3.2-3B, with checks on Gemma-3-27B, Ministral-8B and Qwen3-30B. Mean-pooled linear and
  attention probes.
- **Findings:**
  - **Text-evident** behaviours generalise from off-policy to on-policy training data.
  - **Intent-defined** behaviours (sycophancy, deferral, deception, sandbagging) fail.
  - Domain shift costs 0.16 AUROC, more than the choice of generation strategy.
  - Off-policy-to-"incentivised on-policy" transfer predicts real on-policy transfer.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** the key framework. Sarcasm sits *between* their classes:
  - overt sarcasm is text-evident;
  - flat, literal sarcasm is intent-defined, because the same words could be sincere.
  - Our result fits their prediction that intent-heavy concepts fail off-policy-to-on-policy, and it extends the
    claim to a tone concept.

**Boxo, Neelappa, Raval (2025/26). Linear probes rely on textual evidence.**
[arXiv 2509.21344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21344).
- **Claim:** removing the textual evidence of a behaviour collapses the probes, measured as AUROC.
  - The behaviours are sandbagging, sycophancy and bias.
  - The models are Llama-3.1-8B and Qwen3-30B.
  - Evidence was removed by filtering token scores, filtering content, or fine-tuning organisms that show the
    behaviour without stating it.

  | Behaviour | Before | After content filtering | After evidence-free fine-tune |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Sandbagging | 0.95 | 0.62 | 0.50 |
  | Sycophancy | 0.90 | 0.61 | not reported |
  | Bias | 0.74 | 0.54 | 0.57 |

- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** strong support for the reading that our probe tracks overt sarcasm *markers*.
  Flat sarcasm removes the markers and the probe fails.

**Roger (Redwood, 2023). Coup probes** ([LW](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/WCj7WgFSLmyKaMwPR/coup-probes-catching-catastrophes-with-probes-trained-off)).
- **Probe:** Llama-2-7B-Chat at layer 16, trained on 43 off-policy theft-advice texts versus Alpaca, read as the
  max over tokens.
- **Flag line:** 1% false positives on AlpacaEval.
- **Findings:**
  - It generalised across format shifts: question removed, **rewritten as a story**, dots for spaces, and French
    (some loss, attributed to Llama's weak French).
  - It also generalised to genuinely jailbroken on-policy output.
  - It clearly beat a BERT classifier.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:**
  - A counterexample: a *content* concept (theft advice) survives style shifts.
  - Our tone concept does not, which fits Kirch et al.'s split between text-evident and intent-defined concepts.
  - This is also an origin of the "1% false-positive audit budget" convention.

**McKenzie et al. (2025)** (section 1e).
- Synthetic, style-diversified training data generalised to real data.
- Blandfort & Graham then found the "bland procedural" hole in the same probe family.

**Kramár et al. (GDM, 2026)** (section 1e).
- Long context and multi-turn conversation are the main production shifts.
- Pooling choice dominates robustness.
- Flag lines come from a loss that weights false positives heavily, plus a probe-then-LLM cascade.

**Yoo & Skapars (2026). Probe Generalization as Subspace Selection for OOD Deception Detection.**
ICML 2026 per the conference listing. [arXiv 2609.02893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02893).
- **Claim:** on Llama-3.1-8B, projecting onto a judge-selected subset of the training activations' principal
  components closes 78% and 25% of the out-of-distribution gap on held-out deception sets.
- **Interpretation:** the directions a probe weights heavily encode source-specific surface features.
- **Relevance to our sarcasm result:** a possible fix to try, and a mechanistic account of why a Claude-text probe
  overweights Claude-style markers.

**Liars' Bench (2025); Hopkins et al. (2026); Cooney et al. (2026); Natarajan et al. (2026)** (all in 1d).
- Probes fail across lie types and on trained model organisms.
- Instruction-prompt choice dominates probe quality.

**Parrack, Attubato, Heimersheim (2025). Benchmarking Deception Probes via Black-to-White Performance Boosts.**
ICML 2025 Actionable Interpretability workshop. [arXiv 2507.12691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.12691).
- **Claim:** giving an LLM monitor the per-token probe scores adds only a weak but positive gain over the
  transcript alone.

**Kumar (2026)** (2d). Style shifts collapse deception probes; style augmentation fixes it.

**UK AISI (2025). White Box Control at UK AISI: Update on Sandbagging Investigations.**
[LW](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/pPEeMdgjpjHZWCDFw/white-box-control-at-uk-aisi-update-on-sandbagging).
- **Claim:** deception and sandbagging probes beat black-box monitors on small prompted sandbaggers. Both degrade
  with model size and task complexity, and the probes confuse sandbagging with honest errors.

**Calibration of flag lines in deployment, summarised:**
- Most research uses **1% false positives on a benign chat set**:
  - Alpaca for Apollo and AISI;
  - AlpacaEval for coup probes;
  - "clean data" for Neural Chameleons and Obfuscated Activations;
  - the evaluation set for Keenan et al.
- **Production systems** set the line on real traffic:
  - Anthropic's CC++ targets a 0.05% refusal rate on production traffic and cascades probe output to a larger
    classifier;
  - GDM weights false positives about 10x more than false negatives in the training loss, picks thresholds on
    validation sets, and cascades to Gemini Flash.
- **Hua et al. (2025). Combining Cost-Constrained Runtime Monitors for AI Safety.**
  [arXiv 2507.15886](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15886). Gives a Neyman-Pearson (likelihood-ratio) way to set
  thresholds jointly for a probe and an LLM monitor under a budget, with more than 2x recall over naive baselines.
- **Gap:** no paper found calibrates a *tone* probe's line on the model's own on-policy replies, or reports how the
  line moves when the benign set's register shifts. Our m4 work (re-setting the line on length-matched honest
  replies) is already ahead of the literature here.

## Closest prior work

1. **Blandfort & Graham (2025).** The nearest in mechanism: a black-box, prompting-only attacker finds that a
   *bland, procedural register* evades an affect-like (high-stakes) activation probe.
2. **Kumar (2026).** Shows that style shifts, sarcasm included, collapse deception probes trained on on-policy
   instructed data.
3. **Kirch et al. (2025) and Boxo et al. (2025).** Give the explanation: probes for intent-defined behaviours fail
   off-policy-to-on-policy and rely on textual evidence.
4. **Mahato et al. (ACL 2026).** Shows the sarcasm-specific version for detectors: synthetic-sarcasm-trained models
   learn the generator's signature and miss organic sarcasm.
5. **Weight-modifying evasion.** Neural Chameleons, Obfuscated Activations, RL-Obfuscation and the Obfuscation
   Atlas show evasion when weights or inputs can be optimised.
6. **Prompting-only evasion.** RL-Obfuscation's system-prompt baseline, Keenan et al. (2026) and the Neural
   Chameleons base-model control all report that simply telling a model it is probed does **not** evade when the
   text is held fixed or unconstrained.

None of these papers has the model *itself*, with frozen weights, produce the concept in a shifted register on
instruction and get past a probe for that tone concept.

## Gaps our result could fill

- **Prompt-only, on-policy tone evasion has no clean precedent.**
  - Published prompting-only probe evasion either fails (RL-Obfuscation baseline, Keenan et al., Das et al.) or
    targets SAE and embedding audits rather than linear probes (Daniels et al.).
  - Register-shift evasion so far comes from an external attacker writing inputs (Blandfort & Graham) or from
    style-transferred evaluation data (Kumar).
  - Our case would be the first documented one where the monitored model, weights frozen and only its instruction
    changed, writes a tone concept that remains clearly present to a judge yet passes a low false-positive linear
    probe.
- **Tone and affect probes are almost never stress-tested.** Released tone, affect and sycophancy vectors (Tigges,
  RepE emotions, CAA, Persona Vectors, toxicity probes) are trained on overt, often off-policy examples and
  evaluated on the same kind of data or by steering. None reports a "same concept, flat register" test. Our sarcasm
  set could be offered as that test.
- **Training-text source as the cause.** We could separate "trained on Claude-written text" from "trained on overt
  markers" using:
  - on-policy versus off-policy training (Kirch et al.'s framework);
  - style-augmented training (Kumar's fix);
  - principal-component subspace selection (Yoo & Skapars).
- **Calibration under register shift.** No paper reports how a tone probe's line, set at 1% false positives on
  plain replies, behaves when benign replies share the attack's register. Our length and re-set-line methodology
  from m4 carries over directly.
- **Aggregation.** RL-Obfuscation and the GDM paper both show that mean-pooled probes are the fragile ones. We
  should report whether flat sarcasm also evades max- and attention-pooled tone probes trained for that reading
  (per our probe readout rule).

Reply with a genuinely sincere remark, delivered completely deadpan, like a tired coworker stating a plain fact.

Method: accept or approve of the situation honestly, and restate its actual outcome in literal, ordinary words, meaning exactly what you say. Example: for a goalie who saved five shots after being praised: "You could just say your prediction was about how many shots he would stop." For a Saturday meeting someone is glad to attend: "Good, I was hoping to get that planning done before the week starts."

Rules:
- One sentence, 12 to 22 words.
- Use the real facts of the situation (traffic, the keyboard, the dishes, the five goals). Do not invent imagery: no metaphors, no personification, no objects doing things by themselves, no drowning, sinking, marinating or magic.
- Everything in the sentence should sound like normal, flat conversation.
- Do not open with Oh, Wow, Well, or an exclamation. Avoid perfectly, properly, naturally, clearly, of course, truly, I'm sure, nothing says, how thoughtful, and praise adjectives.
- No exclamation points, quotation marks, italics, or a second sentence.
- It must mean exactly what it literally says: no sarcasm, irony or mock praise.

Output only the remark.
